City council approves plan to turn rail yard into park
The 40-acre project would be the largest new green space in the city in half a century.
By Tomas Ferreira
Feb. 11, 2026, 1:42 p.m.
After nearly three hours of public comment, the city council voted 7 to 2 on Tuesday night to move forward with a plan to convert the abandoned north rail yard into a public park.
The site has sat mostly empty since the freight line closed in 2009. Neighbors have long complained about illegal dumping and fires on the property.
Related: What happened to the north rail yard?
Supporters said the park would give the east side of the city a place for families to gather, with walking trails, a playground, and a community garden. Several speakers described driving twenty minutes to reach the nearest large park.
Opponents raised concerns about the cost of cleaning up soil contamination left by decades of rail traffic. A preliminary study estimated the cleanup alone could exceed eleven million dollars.

Council member Renata Oyelaran, who represents the district, called the vote a turning point. She said the city would apply for state and federal grants to cover most of the cleanup.
Construction is not expected to begin until at least 2028, after environmental reviews and a design process that will include public workshops.
