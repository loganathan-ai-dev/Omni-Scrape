Slow-Roasted Tomato Soup That Tastes Like August

Posted by Dana Whitfield on September 4, 2025 · 48 comments

Jump to Recipe Print Recipe

Every year there is a week in late summer when the tomatoes on my counter outnumber the people in my house. This soup is what happens that week. Roasting the tomatoes low and slow concentrates their sweetness and takes the sharp edge off the garlic.

You do not need perfect tomatoes for this. Split, bruised, or slightly wrinkled ones work beautifully, because the oven does most of the work.

Why roast instead of simmer?

Simmering raw tomatoes gives you a bright, thin soup. Roasting caramelizes the natural sugars and drives off water, so the finished soup is thicker and deeper in flavor without any cream.

I roast the garlic in its skin alongside the tomatoes. It turns soft and jammy and blends straight into the pot.

Ingredients

  • Three pounds of ripe tomatoes, halved
  • One whole head of garlic
  • Two tablespoons of olive oil
  • A few sprigs of fresh thyme
  • Two cups of vegetable stock

Method

Heat the oven to 300 degrees. Arrange the tomatoes cut side up on a sheet pan, tuck the garlic and thyme between them, and drizzle everything with oil.

Roast for about ninety minutes, until the tomatoes have slumped and their edges are browned. Squeeze the garlic out of its skin, then blend everything with the stock until smooth.

Taste for salt, thin with a splash of water if needed, and serve with grilled cheese.
